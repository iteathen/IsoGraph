# One light-causal connection, different emergent distances: causal center and relativity

**Research date:** 2026-10-12  
**Project:** Causal Step  
**Status:** exploratory, AI-assisted consistency analysis of a human-originated thought experiment. Not a demonstrated reduction of relativity, a new cosmological measurement, or a proof of a fundamental discrete light link.  
**Researcher's explicit definitions preserved:** (i) reality is largely emergent from simple conditions (premise); (ii) “light speed” is a **new, non-Einsteinian unit named causal step** (working premise and deliberate terminology), not conventional c; (iii) **time means a sequence of causal events**, not seconds or a background clock (terminological clarification).  
**Current question:** how can an uninterrupted causal light connection from an enormously distant source to an observer count as one causal step, while the same is true of a nearby bulb? Can special/general relativity provide a consistent emergent description? The author additionally distinguishes a pre-spatial **causal center** from an ordinary spatial center and a beginning in causal-event succession from a conventional time coordinate.

## 1. Correcting the previous agent interpretation: a causal center is a genuine candidate

The researcher is entitled to define a **causal center** without first defining spatial geometry. If the underlying event relation has a unique originating condition o from which all events causally descend, then o is a common **ancestral root**. Its privileged status is causal, not a location in three-dimensional space.

Illustrative formalization, **not an adopted axiom**:

```
Events E, causal precedence "≺", origin o ∈ E.
For every e in the connected generated domain other than o:
    o ≺ e.
```

This is an intrinsic root/causal-center property if the event and relation are genuinely part of the theory. It does **not** force o to map to a privileged vertex in an emergent spatial graph, nor does it force matter to cluster around a spatial radius. It does establish a privileged **common ancestry** in a single-origin model.

**Causal time** means ordering by the occurrence/dependence of causal events. Hence a beginning at o is a first event/condition within causal time even if no duration in seconds or spatial coordinates has emerged. An observer's later conventional clock time may be a derived quantity, but is not the researcher's primitive definition.

## 2. The user's proposed one-step light transmission

The thought experiment treats each single uninterrupted source-to-reception light transmission as **one causal step**, whether from a bulb across a room or an arbitrarily distant emitter. The observer is directly influenced by the emission, and the user proposes to count that relation as one unit.

Define a candidate **light-transmission relation** L(e,d) between emission event e and detection event d. Then:

```
L(e_near, d_near) = one causal light link
L(e_far,  d_far ) = one causal light link
```

Their equal link counts do not imply equal **emergent spatial distances**, equal recorded clock histories, equal redshift, or equal attenuation. Critically, calling a *whole transmission* one edge is a proposed way of representing it; it is **not already proved to be exactly one irreducible microscopic update**.

The central unresolved distinction: **direct causal linkage is not necessarily equivalent to one elementary rewriting event**. If one asserts they coincide, a microscopic model must demonstrate it. The researcher has supplied an explicit proposal, not yet its derivation.

**Most faithful initial candidate:** C is a more informative **analytical formulation** of the user's A, not an adopted extra premise. It retains one source-to-eye connection while allowing different global causal configurations to produce different measured separation.

## 4. A relational radar example using *only causal events*

Consider three events in a two-way signal experiment:

```
S --direct light-link--> M --direct light-link--> R
|                                       ^
|---- clock tick 1 -> ... -> tick N ----|
```

- **S:** an observer sends a signal.
- **M:** a distant reflector returns the signal.
- **R:** the original observer receives it.
- **Clock ticks:** a local physical oscillator, whose changes are themselves causal events, experiences N successive events between S and R.

Under the researcher's proposed counting, the two light transmissions are **two** light links for either a nearby or very distant reflector. Nonetheless the *observer's causal clock path* between S and R may contain very different numbers of events:

```
Near experiment: N = small
Far experiment:  N = large
Light transmissions in both: 2
```

No external seconds/minutes are required. There is no contradiction because the event graph may contain **more than one causal path between the same endpoints**, with different lengths, or distinct incomparable events. The reflector event M need not sit on the observer's local clock-tick chain; in a lightlike idealization, intermediate ticks on the observer's worldline need not be causally comparable with M.

A candidate **radar distance** could be constructed from the causal tick count N and other relations. An illustrative normalized expression might be `D_radar / unit_length ≈ N/2`, but this is **only a proposed operational definition** after a calibration/metric-construction rule; the numerical factor and unit of length have not been derived from the two Causal Step premises.

**Caution:** The direct light edges must **not** naively serve as unweighted shortest paths defining three-dimensional spatial distance, or distant light-connected events would collapse to the same graph distance as nearby ones. The relation defining emitted-to-received light links and the relation defining spatial neighborhoods must be kept separate until a justified emergence map connects them.

## 5. What special relativity actually supplies

The standard special-relativistic **null interval** relation for light traveling along an appropriate flat-spacetime path is:

```
c² (Δt)² − (Δx)² = 0,
or ds² = 0.
```

Here c is the **conventional** physical speed-of-light constant of the *emergent comparison theory*, **not** the user's primitive causal-step unit. The null relation holds for both a room-sized light journey and a distant unobstructed light journey, while the conventional coordinate differences may be very different.

This is a structural analogy favorable to the researcher's idea: the *classification* of a lightlike relationship is invariant despite vastly different measured separations. However, **zero spacetime interval is not mathematically the same as one graph edge**. A null geodesic can contain continuous intermediate points; a claimed primitive one-edge interpretation would be an extra dynamical hypothesis. Nor is it correct to invoke a photon's own inertial frame: massless particles have no physical rest frame.

Special relativity therefore illustrates the consistent coexistence of:
- one common causal **type** (null propagation);
- very different measurable travel histories;
- no uniquely privileged global clock.

It does **not** derive the fundamental causal-step axiom for this project.

## 6. Why general relativity is also relevant at large distances

When the separation is cosmological, the expansion of space and gravitational geometry alter how conventional elapsed clock readings, distance and redshift correspond. In a spatially flat expanding Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker model, a radial light ray is null:

```
ds² = −c² dt² + a(t)² dχ² = 0

χ = c ∫ from t_em to t_obs [dt / a(t)]
1 + z = a(t_obs) / a(t_em).
```

These equations belong to an **emergent macroscopic general-relativistic model**. The metric, scale factor a, and standard time coordinate t must be derived or separately introduced by correspondence; they are **not primitive causal time** in Causal Step. Light remains on a null trajectory while the measured redshift and distance depend on cosmic history.

Therefore:
- **special relativity** already explains how all unobstructed lightlike signals share a null classification yet yield different observer-measured separations;
- **general relativity** is needed for realistic propagation across an expanding or strongly curved universe.

Reference: [NASA/IPAC cosmological-distance review by David Hogg](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/Hogg/Hogg4.html); [NASA: How Big Is Space? (2025)](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/astrophysics/how-big-is-space-we-asked-a-nasa-expert-episode-61/).

**100-billion-light-year caution:** NASA estimates the observable universe at about 92 billion light-years in *present-day diameter*, i.e., about 46 billion in present-day radius. A point presently 100 billion light-years away in that specific distance convention would be outside our observable region; other distance definitions, such as *luminosity distance*, may exceed those numbers for actually visible sources. The user's hypothetical compares near/far connections and does not rely on an observationally possible naked-eye source at precisely that distance.

## 7. Direct relevant prior research: causal sets and causal versus spatial distances

Causal set theory studies a locally finite partial order of events. The order represents causal precedence, the count of elements gives a discreteness/volume scale, and the lengths of appropriately selected longest event chains approximate **timelike intervals** under suitable embedding assumptions. Links in causal sets are cover relations; they tend to be associated with near-lightcone structure, which is *not* Euclidean nearest-neighbor proximity.

This is particularly close to the researcher's desired separation: causally related events with near-null separation need not be “near” in a spatial coordinate sense; measured spatial distance requires a more elaborate reconstruction than counting direct links.

References:

## 8. Open tests and research assessment

To advance without smuggling in conventional time and spatial distance as primitives:

1. **Exact link definition:** Which pairs of emission/detection events count as a single causal step, and why is that relation elementary rather than macroscopic?
2. **Observer clocks:** What physical causal-event chains play the role of repeatable clocks? Which chain measures elapsed causal succession, and under what conditions do observers agree?
3. **Relational spatial geometry:** Can a distance function be recovered from event-order relations, signal-exchange histories and/or graph overlaps without measuring spatial distance beforehand?
4. **Frame consistency:** Can differing observer descriptions preserve the same causal relations and null connectivity in a suitable large-scale limit?
5. **Curved/expanding situations:** Can redshift, signal-delay, gravitational lensing and cosmological distances arise from network transformations?
6. **Causal center:** If a single originating event/root exists, can the root be identified from causal ancestry without projecting it into a physically privileged spatial center?
7. **Signal propagation locality:** Does assigning single direct links across arbitrary emergent distances introduce unwanted instantaneous control, or can link semantics be restricted to null-causal transport consistent with a derived effective light cone?
8. **Competing models:** Compare a direct-light-link model, a local elementary-update model, and a layered link-plus-clock-chain model; do not select or “prove” one solely because it was presented first.

**Provisional answer:** The user's near/far one-step example is **not automatically inconsistent** with an emergent relativistic world. One must separate a primitive or effective **light-causal connection** from emergent **spatial distance**, **clock-history length**, and **large-scale metric dynamics**. Special relativity supplies the null-type analogy; general relativity supplies the expanding-curved macroscopic correspondence. The decisive unresolved issue is the model's semantics of **exactly one causal step**, and deriving distances from independent causal histories without circularly presuming space or traditional time.
