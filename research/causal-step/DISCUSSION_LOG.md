**Researcher-originated hypotheses and questions (summary):** Starting from the two previously recorded premises, asks whether the generating reality has (i) one nonspatial beginning, (ii) multiple beginnings, (iii) an atemporal all-possibilities structure, or (iv) indefinitely many generative acts with only causally connected/stable sectors visible to observers. **Agent-assisted research analysis (summary, not verbatim):** A causal ancestry root is not equivalent to a position in emergent physical space. The primitive structure may be a partial order without universal simultaneity, have one/many/no minimal elements, or be an atemporal formal possibility set. The Ruliad as Wolfram describes it is a limit over rules, initial conditions and possible evaluations, with branching *and merging*; it should not be equated with proven quantum execution of all universes. A spatial graph, causal event graph and multiway/rulial graph are distinct objects. Emergent physical dimension can be tested by ball-volume growth V(r) ∝ r^3 in an appropriately defined spatial relation, unlike free-branching trees with exponential growth. The presence of a common causal ancestor alone does **not** imply a privileged center of matter in a later spatial slice; that result would require a specified emergent metric, persistent privileged location and matter-production/density dynamics. Standard Big Bang cosmology does not posit an explosion from one point in preexisting space. No premise defining a unit of causal step in standard distance/time units has been added.

## 2026-10-12 — A causal center and the one-link near/far light puzzle

**Researcher (verbatim):**

> Right. So when we talk about the origin of the emergent universe, I'm talking about the causal step origin. So you can say, well, space hasn't even emerged yet, so you can't say it has a center. Well, yes I can. It has a causal center. Well, you might say that time hasn't emerged yet, so you can't say that it originated in a certain time. Well, I'd say, well, yes I can. It's causal step time. So that's how we need to be thinking of this. Now, let's look at what we see in the universe and try to match it up with what this graph might produce, okay? So we see, let's say we have a distant light source, a hundred billion light years away, and we can see it with our naked eye. So in a sense, that's one causal step: light straight from the light source straight to my eye. Okay, but how do we make that jive against the hundred billion light years when I could also define the light from the light bulb in my room as one causal step, yet it is far less than a hundred billion light years away? So relativity must come into play here, and I think it's, I think it— what is it? Is it general relativity or is it special relativity? I can't remember. But one of those two has to come into play here to describe this effect, otherwise we have an inconsistency. So how might that look?

**Researcher-originated clarification and hypothesis:** A unique early causal condition can constitute a **causal center**, not a conventional point in pre-existing spatial geometry. An earliest causal step constitutes a beginning in **causal-event sequence time**, not external seconds. The investigator proposes that uninterrupted emission-to-detection light can be treated as **one causal step**, whether the light source is across a room or enormously distant under a later emergent distance measure. He asks how observed near/far differences could consistently arise, and whether special or general relativity supplies an analogy or correspondence. The numerical hundred-billion-light-year source is an illustrative hypothetical, not an observationally validated naked-eye object or specified cosmological distance definition.

**Assistant scientific comparison (summary, not verbatim):** A causal ancestry root is a coherent candidate for causal *centrality*, even when space has no privileged center. Special relativity classifies both near and far unobstructed light paths as **null** (spacetime interval zero), but this does **not establish** they are literally one fundamental rewriting step. General relativity is necessary to model propagation in an expanding or curved cosmic geometry, where observable distances and redshifts differ. A possible relational graph distinguishes an emission-to-detection light link from the causal-event chain counted by an observer's physical clock: a near/far two-way signal may use the same two light links while the observer's local tick-event chain between send and return contains very different numbers of events. Spatial distance must emerge from the full relation structure, not unweighted shortest path along all light links. One proposed light-link semantics is still an **open modeling question**. The official NASA 2025 estimate of observable universe diameter is approximately 92 billion light-years, so a 100-billion-light-year present-day radial spatial distance is beyond the standard observable horizon; this does not invalidate the near/far thought experiment.
