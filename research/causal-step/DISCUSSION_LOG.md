## 2026-10-12 — Causal beginning(s), Ruliad possibilities, and emergent spatial origin

**Researcher (verbatim):**

> So if all reality emerged from a simple set of conditions, would it emerge from a singularity? Or would it emerge from— see, there's no space, so there's no idea of where in the beginning, because space itself is supposed to be emergent. So would it— let's assume that it emerges from some form of bizarre quantum effect, where all possibilities are being played out. Would it emerge from once, or many times simultaneously? Would it emerge infinitely, but only the things that survive interaction are visible to us? I mean, if we take Stephen Wolfram's idea of it, and I think it's the best formulation of it so far, all possibilities are played out, but we only see the ones that we're connected to. So we're in an area of the Ruliad where we can see and interact and make determinations. But theoretically there are many other branches of the Ruliad, which we don't exist on and we will never relate to. So maybe everything played out simultaneously. How does that translate to three-dimensional space? And if there was a beginning in a time sense, then it would be a causal step beginning. And if there's a causal step beginning, then wouldn't that mean that matter in this universe would be concentrated around the origin of that causal step? What do you think of that reasoning?

**Researcher-originated hypotheses and questions (summary):** Starting from the two previously recorded premises, asks whether the generating reality has (i) one nonspatial beginning, (ii) multiple beginnings, (iii) an atemporal all-possibilities structure, or (iv) indefinitely many generative acts with only causally connected/stable sectors visible to observers. Stephen Wolfram's Ruliad is proposed as a promising reference for observer-conditioned access to possible computations. The researcher explicitly rejects presupposing a spatial "where" before geometry emerges and wonders whether a first **causal-step** beginning entails spatial concentration of matter. These are **open questions and tentative analogies**, not additional adopted premises or established findings.

**Agent-assisted research analysis (summary, not verbatim):** A causal ancestry root is not equivalent to a position in emergent physical space. The primitive structure may be a partial order without universal simultaneity, have one/many/no minimal elements, or be an atemporal formal possibility set. The Ruliad as Wolfram describes it is a limit over rules, initial conditions and possible evaluations, with branching *and merging*; it should not be equated with proven quantum execution of all universes. A spatial graph, causal event graph and multiway/rulial graph are distinct objects. Emergent physical dimension can be tested by ball-volume growth V(r) ∝ r^3 in an appropriately defined spatial relation, unlike free-branching trees with exponential growth. The presence of a common causal ancestor alone does **not** imply a privileged center of matter in a later spatial slice; that result would require a specified emergent metric, persistent privileged location and matter-production/density dynamics. Standard Big Bang cosmology does not posit an explosion from one point in preexisting space. No premise defining a unit of causal step in standard distance/time units has been added.
