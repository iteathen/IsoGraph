**Researcher-originated clarification and hypothesis:** A unique early causal condition can constitute a **causal center**, not a conventional point in pre-existing spatial geometry. An earliest causal step constitutes a beginning in **causal-event sequence time**, not external seconds. The investigator proposes that uninterrupted emission-to-detection light can be treated as **one causal step**, whether the light source is across a room or enormously distant under a later emergent distance measure. **Assistant scientific comparison (summary, not verbatim):** A causal ancestry root is a coherent candidate for causal *centrality*, even when space has no privileged center. Special relativity classifies both near and far unobstructed light paths as **null** (spacetime interval zero), but this does **not establish** they are literally one fundamental rewriting step. General relativity is necessary to model propagation in an expanding or curved cosmic geometry, where observable distances and redshifts differ. A possible relational graph distinguishes an emission-to-detection light link from the causal-event chain counted by an observer's physical clock: a near/far two-way signal may use the same two light links while the observer's local tick-event chain between send and return contains very different numbers of events. Spatial distance must emerge from the full relation structure, not unweighted shortest path along all light links. One proposed light-link semantics is still an **open modeling question**. The official NASA 2025 estimate of observable universe diameter is approximately 92 billion light-years, so a 100-billion-light-year present-day radial spatial distance is beyond the standard observable horizon; this does not invalidate the near/far thought experiment.
