## Question and scope

What must be counted as primitive when a productive, effectively presented symbolic formal system has a very small visible syntax but relies on a parser, evaluator, state discipline, application structure, rule-use conditions, or readout? The manuscript argues that a **complete comparison of productive apparatus**, called *fair accounting* in this historical source, must identify three dependency questions:

- **α:** what supplies effective transition or productive difference?
- **β:** what supplies determinate formal presence, state, object, or result?
- **γ:** what determines how transition bears on presence?

These are **questions/role families**, not a fixed ontology, three separate devices, or a requirement for exactly three alphabetic glyphs. A single mechanism may answer several questions, depending on the formal presentation and the scope of comparison.

## 2026-10-12 adversarial review and researcher correction

**Current reviewed disposition:** the manuscript's **unique irreducible three-role minimum is unproved**. Its most defensible contribution is a complete-dependency comparison method that counts parsers, execution, rule application, conventions, and readout instead of equating a tiny printed alphabet with a complete productive system. The SKI encoding illustrates operational representability, **not** a physical ontology or an independently proved three-role lower bound.

The researcher identified the deeper original concern as **self-reference and semantic self-grounding**: investigators, measurements, and the semantic language used to describe a universe are themselves within that universe. An internally complete formalism may face a recurring question of how it certifies correspondence to the underlying physical world. This is an important research hypothesis **but is not yet a proved Gödel-style limitation on physical theories**; Gödel and Tarski results have additional technical assumptions. Keep distinct the manuscript's local definitional/independence problem and the broader reflexive-validation problem.

That assertion is **not** an independently checked theorem or qualified IsoGraph result merely because the manuscript has been archived.

The paper does not establish that the universe itself is an effective formal system or that α/β/γ are physical constituents.

## Significant boundaries and potential falsifiers

1. Alphabet size, computational universality, and primitive productive-role sufficiency are not interchangeable claims.
2. The roles cannot be declared genuinely independent by naming them; the minimality proof must survive a candidate in which one primitive relation appears to perform all three functions.
3. Reducing visible notation can transfer productive requirements into counted auxiliary apparatus instead of eliminating them.
4. A claim about effectively presented symbolic systems cannot automatically be extended to non-effective mathematics, ontology, or the origin of physical reality.
5. A complete comparison must state a precise equivalence criterion. Different encodings and implementations may preserve behavior under one equivalence while differing in structurally relevant ways under another.
