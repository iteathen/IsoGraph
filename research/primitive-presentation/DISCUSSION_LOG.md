## 2026-10-12 — Researcher's self-reference diagnosis

**Researcher (verbatim):**

> Yeah, I found circularity is one way of calling it, but I think circularity is not the correct way of describing it. I think it's a self-reference problem, which is a form of circularity, but it has broader implications. The problem I found at the bottom of my study of primitive representation and how it relates to what is real. So this would be, I mean, in physics you've got, you know, broadly two different categories of things being worked on. You've got the reality that you're taking your measurements from, and you've got your semantic layer of mathematics that's describing it. And the thought was that if you have a sufficiently primitive representation of the semantics, its structure should probably mirror something in reality. And I still think that's true, but I was trying to demonstrate it. And I came very close, I think. But what I ultimately found is that I hit a Gödel-style self-reference undeterminability kind of problem. And it surfaces in circular argument. So looking at it as a circular argument and presenting it that way would diminish the finding, even though the finding is one of self-reference and will be inconclusive. But the bigger picture is that if you do find something, a primitive source of reality or physics, physical reality, you're likely going to run into a Gödel problem, because as integrated agents of this physical reality, you and I, we cannot be the measure of our own measuring stick. So we will run into a Gödel-style problem at some point if we uncover enough rules of reality. We will find one that has Gödel-style properties to it. And so when I run... you know, I stopped doing primitive research because at the bottom of it is a self-reference problem, and you'll never get past it until some part of you can step outside of reality to measure it. So what you're seeing as a circular argument is a reflection of the deeper problem in this kind of research.

**Researcher-originated interpretation:** The historical aim was to identify an invariant structural correspondence between primitive symbolic semantics and the physical reality responsible for the measurements. A perceived self-reference / self-grounding boundary motivated discontinuing that primitive research direction. The researcher considers this boundary a more consequential finding than the local circularity label.

**Assistant response (research summary, not verbatim):** Distinguish an unproved role-independence theorem from a potential self-grounding problem. Investigators, their instruments, and the formalism itself reside inside the physical reality being modeled. This gives a real epistemological and semantical issue but is not, without further assumptions, a direct application of Gödel incompleteness. Precise avenues include Tarski-style semantic truth boundaries, conditional diagonal self-prediction constructions, and impossibility of distinguishing observationally equivalent generators under a specified experiment class. The paper's extant §15/§19/§22 already acknowledges pieces of this; the researcher's contribution is to identify the self-reference boundary as central to the failed broader research ambition rather than a peripheral caveat.

**Status:** Substantive scientific/philosophical conjecture and retrospective research history; **no proof of a physical impossibility theorem or universal Gödel obstruction**. Review and comparison: [critical review](reviews/2026-10-12-critical-review-and-self-reference.md).
