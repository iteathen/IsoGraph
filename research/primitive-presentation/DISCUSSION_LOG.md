**Researcher (verbatim):**

> Yeah, I found circularity is one way of calling it, but I think circularity is not the correct way of describing it. The problem I found at the bottom of my study of primitive representation and how it relates to what is real. So this would be, I mean, in physics you've got, you know, broadly two different categories of things being worked on. But what I ultimately found is that I hit a Gödel-style self-reference undeterminability kind of problem. And it surfaces in circular argument. But the bigger picture is that if you do find something, a primitive source of reality or physics, physical reality, you're likely going to run into a Gödel problem, because as integrated agents of this physical reality, you and I, we cannot be the measure of our own measuring stick. So we will run into a Gödel-style problem at some point if we uncover enough rules of reality. We will find one that has Gödel-style properties to it. And so when I run... you know, I stopped doing primitive research because at the bottom of it is a self-reference problem, and you'll never get past it until some part of you can step outside of reality to measure it. So what you're seeing as a circular argument is a reflection of the deeper problem in this kind of research.

**Researcher-originated interpretation:** The historical aim was to identify an invariant structural correspondence between primitive symbolic semantics and the physical reality responsible for the measurements. A perceived self-reference / self-grounding boundary motivated discontinuing that primitive research direction. The researcher considers this boundary a more consequential finding than the local circularity label.

**Assistant response (research summary, not verbatim):** Distinguish an unproved role-independence theorem from a potential self-grounding problem. Investigators, their instruments, and the formalism itself reside inside the physical reality being modeled. This gives a real epistemological and semantical issue but is not, without further assumptions, a direct application of Gödel incompleteness. Precise avenues include Tarski-style semantic truth boundaries, conditional diagonal self-prediction constructions, and impossibility of distinguishing observationally equivalent generators under a specified experiment class. The paper's extant §15/§19/§22 already acknowledges pieces of this; the researcher's contribution is to identify the self-reference boundary as central to the failed broader research ambition rather than a peripheral caveat.

**Status:** Substantive scientific/philosophical conjecture and retrospective research history; **no proof of a physical impossibility theorem or universal Gödel obstruction**. Review and comparison: [critical review](reviews/2026-10-12-critical-review-and-self-reference.md).

And then I thought, well, why should it be that circularity will always reoccur? And I found the broader idea, and that is: at this scale, you are now trying to represent all reality itself. So the direction I was going was going to dead-end at Gödel-style boundaries. I'm sorry if you can't see that, but I encourage you to adopt a little bit of flexibility in your thinking. Extend Gödel's theory to all of formalizable systems, and then you can start to see that as agents of a theoretically formalizable system, God forbid it is not a formalizable system—we are in trouble if it is not. So every indication is that our reality is a formalizable system with a finite set of rules. And there are reasons why we should believe that. But if it is a formalizable system, which it likely is, then it's bounded by Gödel-style restrictions. And so at some point, when you uncover the rules of reality, you're going to run against this boundary of continuously running into circular arguments. It's a sign that you've hit the bottom. You've hit the edge. That's why I couldn't avoid it.

**Researcher-originated idea / interpretation:** Repeated circularity was first identified in the Primitive Presentation program. The researcher then proposed a more general explanation: approaching a purported complete and self-including representation of physical reality might force Gödel-like formal or physical self-reference limitations, creating a recurrent obstruction to absolute justification. The researcher believes this explains why the attempted primitive-representation strategy met a dead end.

**Assistant research response (summary, not verbatim):** Accepted the broader **physical self-inference** direction as a serious hypothesis rather than reducing it to ordinary proof circularity. Located relevant work by David H. Wolpert (2001/2008) proving impossibility results for embedded physical inference devices under specified assumptions, independent of particular physical laws. Distinguished (i) the possibility of a finite generative description, (ii) decidability of all its implications, and (iii) absolute internal certification of its correspondence to reality. Gödel's incompleteness theorems do not apply to every formally describable system without the necessary assumptions. Some self-reference and total-prediction demands support diagonal impossibility arguments, but this does not imply physical generative rules cannot be discovered.

**Research status and next step:** Candidate self-grounding boundary, **not a proved universal physical Gödel no-go theorem**. The detailed analysis and sources are saved in [reflexive physical inference boundary](reviews/2026-10-12-reflexive-physical-inference-boundary.md); the original lower-bound proof problem remains separately classified in the [adversarial review](reviews/2026-10-12-critical-review-and-self-reference.md).

## 2026-10-12 — Evidential boundary sign and two-symbol complete-system challenge

**Researcher (verbatim):**

> Yeah, when I define something as a sign that you've reached a boundary, that's different than saying you have factually, definitively, and unarguably reached the boundary. Sign means evidence. You have some evidence now to suggest that you have reached a boundary. And since my work was in primitive representation, we can clearly see that I was aiming at that boundary. And now that we were already down to three symbols, and I can demonstrate that it's Turing complete, going to two symbols was the next step. And I found that the most difficult because I could not find a way to do it with two symbols. But furthermore, I could not find a way to prove that it cannot be done with two symbols. So the best I could do is to say that you would need to find a way to fulfill certain roles. But that's not to say that it couldn't be done with two symbols. That's where I got stuck in circularity, because any time you try to prove that it can't be done with two symbols, you're going to run into singularity, unless by some miracle you find a way to represent a Turing complete system with fair accounting. By the way, you don't get to cheat and just say binary is Turing complete. It's not, because you have no way to delineate figures in there. You have no executor. You have left. You've cheated if you call binary a Turing complete system. It's a Turing complete language, but that's leaving out the system that made the Turing completeness possible. So Turing completeness came from a combination of the binary language and a system that ran it. So to complete the circle, to have a Turing complete system, you've got to account for one extra factor at least. And I could not prove that you could not do it with two. Although I am 99.99999% sure that you can't do it with two. But there's no way to prove it. And any time you do, you run into a circular argument. And if you keep coming up with these circular arguments like I did, you start to realize that you have reached a boundary. So I said, that's a sign that you have reached the boundary of what is possible in reality, unless you can step out of reality at least partially so that you can make a comparison.

**Researcher-originated clarification:** “Sign” denotes **evidence**, not conclusive demonstration. The concrete technical obstruction was the move from a known computationally universal three-mark formal presentation to two marks while insisting on complete accounting of parsing, application, execution, interpretation, and readout. The researcher reports that neither a satisfactory two-mark primitive reduction nor a non-circular impossibility proof was found; the repeated blockage motivates the candidate Gödel-style physical self-grounding interpretation. The extremely high stated confidence is a personal judgment, not an independently established probability or mathematical result.

**Assistant analysis (summary, not verbatim):** Distinguished a bare binary string from a two-character **operationally specified system**. Reviewed binary combinatory logic with grammar `Term ::= 00 | 01 | 1 Term Term`, K/S rewrite schemas, exact recursive parser, explicit reduction strategy and output convention. This construct offers a legitimate two-character object language with the additional interpreter/rule apparatus **counted**, not magically assumed absent. Its known universality and bounded examples refute a bare alphabet-count impossibility, **but not** the stronger conjecture that two irreducible productive **role-families** suffice or that a primitive physical account can self-ground. The three-symbol witness must be held to the same complete-apparatus standard. Recurring definitional circularity remains evidence of an obstruction to the attempted proof strategy, but whether this points to a mathematical lower bound, unstable accounting criterion, or physical self-grounding boundary is still unresolved.

**Evidence and next test:** [Counted two-symbol stress test and boundary analysis](reviews/2026-10-12-two-symbol-boundary-and-counted-executor.md); [bounded executable probe](reviews/evidence/binary_combinatory_accounting_probe.py). ## 2026-10-12 — Potential impossibility of absolute proof of the self-grounding boundary

**Researcher (verbatim):**

> I agree. It would be nice to have a more rigorous result. But if what I'm saying is true, you're never going to be able to find it. You got to— If what I'm saying is true, you're going to be up against Gödel-type constraints, and you are always going to find that you can't prove this.

**Researcher-originated clarification:** The proposed boundary is reflexive: if fully internal, unconditional proof of reality's ultimate foundation is subject to Gödel-like self-reference restrictions, demanding a rigorous *absolute* proof of precisely that limit could itself be an impossible requirement. This reinforces the investigator's earlier distinction between a **sign** of reaching a boundary and an independently conclusive proof.

**Assistant research response (substantive summary, not verbatim):** A distinguished metatheory may prove that a specified sufficiently expressive theory cannot establish its own consistency or truth by internal resources. Such relative impossibility proofs do **not** automatically provide self-certifying, unconditional foundations for the metatheory or physical reality as a whole. This makes a conditional limit theorem compatible with the researcher's concern. Strong antecedents include Gödel's scoped incompleteness theorems and David Wolpert's impossibility results for sufficiently general physical inference devices embedded in a universe. The research program should distinguish (1) absolute certification of the entirety of reality, (2) a precise conditional theorem about self-certification within an explicitly defined class, and (3) the evidence that a particular historical proof strategy repeatedly encountered circularity. No claim that the proposed physical impossibility has already been proved.

**Further research obligation:** Define the exact requirement allegedly impossible to prove *within* the represented system, the inference apparatus, its assumed closure and expressive strength, and what counts as an external validating metatheory. Preserve the possibility that mathematical scope-relative boundary theorems can be proved even if unconditional physical self-grounding cannot be.

**Relevant sources:** [Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems (SEP)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/); [David H. Wolpert, Physical limits of inference (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2008.03.040).
