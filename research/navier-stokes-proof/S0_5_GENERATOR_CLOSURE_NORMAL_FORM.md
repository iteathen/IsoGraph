# S0.5 — generator/closure normal form

**Purpose:** separate mechanisms that construct/transform the solution from theorems that certify or close the resulting interfaces.

This is not a new proof claim beyond S0.4. It is a stricter accounting rule for future compression.

## Why this distinction is necessary

A reduced proof can become artificially small by replacing a mechanism with its downstream theorem as an axiom.

Example:

- support coloring plus residual algebra generate an exact labelwise residual decomposition;
- if the decomposition itself is simply postulated, the geometry that makes nonlinear cross-label terms vanish disappears from the explanation.

That is an invalid compression for the present research goal.

The rule is:

> **Compress repeated proofs, not distinct generators or distinct certified properties.**

# IV. Compression rule

For future reduced formulas, an allowed compression may:

- hide repeated coefficient estimates inside one already-proved interface;
- hide repeated stage occurrences behind the recurrence schema;
- hide source implementation details that do not change interface obligations.

It may **not**:

- replace G3 support geometry by simply postulating C2 residual additivity;
- replace G4 covariance realization by simply postulating stress cancellation;
- replace G8 FiveRows construction by simply postulating zero debt;
- replace G11 protected geometry by simply postulating candidate blowup;
- identify C0 flatness with C1 exact exterior zero;
- identify C3 invariant gain with C4 physical residual rate;
- identify C7 whole-space uniqueness with C8 periodic uniqueness.

# V. Status

S0.5 does not reduce the 25 S0.4 atoms by deleting properties.

Instead it assigns them a typed proof role:

- 16 generative/structural mechanisms;
- 9 closure/bridge theorems.

This is the current preferred normal form for understanding and further reduction.

Any future claim of a smaller **generative** basis must show, rather than assume, how the missing G-role is constructed from the retained roles.
