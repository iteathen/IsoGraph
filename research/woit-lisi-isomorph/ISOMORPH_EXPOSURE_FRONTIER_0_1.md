# Isomorph-Exposure Frontier 0.1

**Status:** campaign research method; not IsoGraph semantic authority  
**Originating direction:** Joshua Oshiro, 2026-10-03  
**Formalization:** agent-assisted

## Purpose

The Woit and Lisi source objects are too large to compare extensionally. They must be represented axiomatically.

The abstraction boundary is chosen to expose nontrivial isomorphs while preserving every load-bearing source distinction.

The governing target is:

~~~text
remove presentation-specific structure
until
the generative relational invariant becomes visible

but stop before
removing a distinction that changes reconstruction,
allowed transformations, identity, transition anatomy,
or physical/source role
~~~

This is the **isomorph-exposure frontier**.

## Multi-level representation

For each source formulation F, maintain a chain of lawful projections:

~~~text
F0  source formulation
F1  named mathematical construction
F2  source-specific axiomatic presentation
F3  generative relational invariant
F4  primitive logical support
~~~

The comparison is allowed to inspect several levels.

A bridge may therefore be discovered as:

~~~text
W-F_i at level a
    <-> common quotient K
L-F_j at level b
~~~

without claiming that the two complete formulations are equivalent.

## Continue-or-stop rule

For one abstraction step A -> A':

Continue when:
- source reconstruction remains exact for the declared view;
- no load-bearing distinction is lost;
- representation-specific detail is removed;
- the structural comparison surface becomes more general.

Stop or branch the abstraction when:
- two source objects that must remain distinct become indistinguishable;
- a source transition or forbidden relation disappears;
- a reality/chirality/signature distinction is erased;
- an unresolved QU region is silently resolved;
- a physical/source role needed for reconstruction is projected away without authority.

## Nested bridge schemas

Prefer nested candidate schemas rather than one maximally strong bridge.

Example:

~~~text
weak chiral-action skeleton
    <- stronger Clifford-action refinement
    <- stronger triality refinement
~~~

If Woit instantiates only the weak skeleton while Lisi instantiates the stronger refinement, the common quotient is the weak skeleton and the stronger Lisi structure remains a residual.

This is preferable to declaring "no bridge" because Woit lacks full triality, or declaring "same structure" by deleting triality without recording the residual.

## Generators over endpoints

The preferred abstraction retains the relations that generate the observed structure.

For example, three carriers alone are weak evidence.

A stronger invariant is:

~~~text
three carriers
+ typed bilinear/trilinear incidence
+ grading/chirality
+ involution
+ transformation preserving or cycling roles
~~~

The generator relations are often more bridge-informative than named endpoint objects.

## Reconstruction requirement

Every candidate common quotient K must retain projection records:

~~~text
W source -> W source-specific axioms -> K
L source -> L source-specific axioms -> K
~~~

and later, where claimed:

~~~text
K + W residual -> reconstruct W view
K + L residual -> reconstruct L view
~~~

Failure of upward reconstruction weakens the bridge disposition.

## Nontriviality test

A candidate abstraction is too weak when it survives deletion of nearly every distinguishing relation.

Before promotion, try deleting:
- chirality;
- incidence;
- involution;
- order-three/cyclic structure;
- reality/signature constraints;
- source/target transition roles.

If the purported bridge is unchanged after removing all load-bearing structure, it is probably a trivial common pattern rather than the sought isomorph.
