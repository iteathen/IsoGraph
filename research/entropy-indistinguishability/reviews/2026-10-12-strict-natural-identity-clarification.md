# Strict natural identity — full physical indiscernibility, not limited observation

**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Status:** researcher-originated foundational identity hypothesis; substantive correction to the initial review; **not** an accepted physical theorem or a revision of qualified IsoGraph NEI semantics.  
**Source:** [substantive research conversation](../DISCUSSION_LOG.md).  
**Retrospective distinction:** This does not supersede the original uploaded working note, which is preserved unchanged.

## Central correction to the initial assessment

The researcher's intended antecedent is **much stronger** than "no feasible observer can currently distinguish two processes." It is **no physical distinction exists anywhere, at any time, under any physically possible interaction, even for an investigator knowing every fact of the universe**.

This explicitly includes intrinsic microstructure, spatiotemporal configuration and location (when such notions exist), relational and causal dependencies, all physically meaningful history and downstream consequences, physically allowed counterfactuals, and possible interaction records. It is not a statement about experimental budgets, instrument resolution, lack of observations, or currently unknown variables.

The researcher proposes **natural identity**: when complete physical indiscernibility obtains in this strongest sense, the supposedly separate descriptions denote **the same physical thing/state/history**, rather than two numerically distinct physical realities connected merely by a convenient equivalence chosen for a task.

The initial review characterized "operational equivalence -> physical identity" as a relatively large ontological leap. Under **bounded evidence**, that objection is important. Under the researcher's **stipulated exhaustive physical identity profile**, that description overstates the gap: claiming a further *physically genuine* difference requires specifying some additional distinguishing physical fact, or admitting nonphysical primitive **thisness** that does no causal or observable work. The researcher rejects the latter as surplus individuality.

## Physical identity of indiscernibles — proposed formulation

Let ℱ_phys be a stipulated exhaustive family of meaningful *non-identity-presupposing* physical predicates/relations about the full system, including intrinsic, relational, spatiotemporal, causal, interaction and counterfactual distinctions. The exclusion of identity-naming properties such as "is identical to x" prevents making the criterion tautologically true or false by inserting the conclusion.

As a schematic proposal:

~~~text
x ~natural y
    iff
for every physically meaningful non-identity-presupposing fact F,
    F(x) and F(y) have the same truth value
and every physical relation, multiplicity and admissible continuation
is preserved without remainder.
~~~

**Natural identity principle (candidate):** Where this condition is **fully satisfied**, there is no further independent physical fact that distinguishes x and y. The difference between descriptive labels does not by itself establish two physical entities.

This is **global** in intended scope, not the deliberately scoped relational equivalence of any one instrument or task. It is close to a physically interpreted **identity of indiscernibles** and an explicit refusal to postulate *haecceity* (primitive label-based thisness) as an extra physical property.

**Epistemic and logical boundary:** The criterion is coherent, and when accepted as an axiom/individuation principle it permits quotienting away surplus labels. Yet exact coincidence of all physical facts in all possible contexts generally cannot be confirmed by any finite experiment, and the converse of Leibniz's indiscernibility-of-identicals is contested in philosophy. A formal model can assign distinct domain elements to qualitative duplicates unless its interpretation includes an identity-of-indiscernibles principle; that model's extra indices need not be physical. The principle therefore need not be empirically refutable under the stipulated premise; **absence of a physical falsifier is not by itself a deduction of numerical equality in every ontology**.

## Why standard counterexamples may not reach the user's actual premise

- Two macroscopically indistinguishable balls at **different** locations fail the premise: their spatial and relational facts differ. The user's condition explicitly includes exact position, parts, and causal relations.
- Two identical particles whose exchange is physically undetectable cannot automatically be replaced by **one** particle. Multiplicity, occupation, correlations, and exclusion constraints are potential physical facts. A label-permutation being surplus is not the same as particle-count being surplus.
- Two mathematical representations may be structurally equivalent but still describe different physically realized conditions if any relational/counterfactual fact differs. Exact *complete* natural identity is a much stronger standard than a useful scoped isomorphism.

The user's thought experiment rules out those distinctions by stipulation. It challenges a hypothetical **unobservable numerical label only** once every other fact is exhausted.

## Important consequence for source entropy and histories

The original paper asks whether low- and high-source-entropy *generative histories* can become physically identical. This strong natural-identity framing makes the tense and reference object decisive:

1. **Same final full physical state, different physically real past histories:** These may be the same **current state** in a Markovian description but are not automatically the same **full history** if the histories contain any genuine physical difference. If total past facts are included, the natural-identity premise fails for different histories.
2. **Different ensembles/probability laws:** Shannon entropy is a property of the specified distribution. Different source distributions could lead to distinct intervention/repetition statistics and then be physically distinguishable. If no physically meaningful distributional or counterfactual difference exists either, the distinction between "low" and "high" source entropy must be formulated as a feature of alternative *representations*, not a surviving physical fact.
3. **Different algorithmic descriptions of exactly the same complete physical structure:** If no physical fact can distinguish these descriptions' referents, the proposed natural-identity principle treats their distinction as representational redundancy. This is the cleanest interpretation of the researcher's strong claim.

The source-entropy inequality H(f(S)) ≤ H(S) inside a specified deterministic probabilistic model remains intact. The question is whether differences in the descriptions' randomness budgets correspond to differences **in physical reality**, not whether deterministic functions produce extra Shannon entropy.

## Relation to established work and IsoGraph NEI

- The 2025 [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on the Identity of Indiscernibles](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-indiscernible/) distinguishes numerical identity from qualitative/relational properties and carefully explains how putting singular identity predicates into the universal property set would trivialize the proposal.
- The SEP [Haecceitism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/haecceitism/) entry describes the opposing view that distinctness can remain after every qualitative fact agrees.
- The SEP [Identity and Individuality in Quantum Theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qt-idind/) discusses identity, permutation invariance, physical multiplicity, and ongoing disputes about individuality.
- The IsoGraph [Natural Entropic Identity 0.4](../../../extensions/nei/NATURAL_ENTROPIC_IDENTITY_SPEC_0_4_CANDIDATE.md) discipline distinguishes a *missing observable difference* from positive same-identity evidence, and scoped quotients from global natural identity. The current researcher's premise is **hypothetically complete positive indiscernibility**, not the weaker claim "we have found no difference so far." This discussion is exploratory and **does not modify qualified NEI semantics or declare an NEI SAME result**.

## Updated assessment

The researcher is correct to reject the earlier shorthand "practically indistinguishable" and "identity is a large leap" for their stated premise. In this strongest form, the proposal is a coherent and parsimonious principle: **do not multiply physical identities after exhausting all physically meaningful differences**.

What remains unsettled is not whether a limited observer can be fooled; it is (a) what qualifies as all *genuine* physical facts, (b) whether numerical individuality can be primitive even without such a fact, and (c) whether two cases with truly different random generative histories can satisfy the exhaustive premise.

This research result is a refined **position**, not an empirical proof of natural identity or the absence of all primitive thisness. It does, however, shift the burden of explanation onto anyone positing distinct physical existents when they concede that there is no physically meaningful difference to distinguish them.
