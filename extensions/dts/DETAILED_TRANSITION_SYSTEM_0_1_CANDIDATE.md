# IsoGraph Detailed Transition System Extension — 0.1 Candidate

**Status:** unqualified normative extension candidate  
**Short name:** DTS 0.1  
**Author:** Joshua Oshiro  
**Repository:** `iteathen/IsoGraph`  
**Core dependency:** qualified cumulative Core 0.17 + Core 0.18 observation-first authority; Core 0.18 is qualified at the exact artifact `CORE_SPEC_DRAFT_0_18_OBSERVATION_FIRST_CANDIDATE.md`  
**QU dependency:** qualified QU 0.1 semantics are a required DTS semantic dependency; a determinate transition need not instantiate a QU region  
**NEI dependency:** qualified NEI 0.4 only when a DTS claim actually depends on natural/domain identity or distinctness  
**Discovery dependency:** none for DTS truth; qualified Discovery Protocols 0.1–0.6 may consume DTS/TI structure but never supply DTS proof authority  
**Integrated dependency status:** Core 0.18 + QU 0.1 + NEI 0.4 + DP 0.1–0.6 passed Experiment 019; DTS remains outside that qualified composition until separately qualified  
**Native vocabulary:** `DTS_VOCAB_0_1.isg` / `DTS_NATIVE_VOCAB_0_1.md`  
**Growth rule:** adds no Core parser syntax, identifier system, boundary system, uncertainty system, canonical factorization, execution mechanism, or universal transition ontology

DTS fills one representational gap:

```text
IsoGraph Core: detailed structure/state
DTS:           detailed change between such structures
```

The extension is deliberately small. Mechanism profiles such as DPO rewriting, sequential execution, concurrency, cost accounting, optimization, and structural-signature filtering remain separately versioned work above DTS base semantics.

---

# 0. Interpretation barriers

Do not collapse these distinctions:

```text
transition
    != mechanism

observed change
    != known cause

same source and target states
    != same transition
    != Transition Isomorph (TI)

role factorization
    != invariant decomposition

one valid decomposition
    != canonical decomposition

missing transition detail
    != empty transition detail

no QU instance
    != proof that no unresolved structure exists

unknown transition structure
    != irrelevant transition structure

TI
    != same mechanism
    != same rule
    != same action
    != same decomposition
    != same cost
    != same temporal realization
    != NEI SAME

profile qualification
    != DTS base qualification
```

These barriers are normative.

---

# 1. Mission

DTS represents transitions as first-class, structurally inspectable relations between ordinary IsoGraph structures.

The governing shape is:

```text
G- --tau--> G+
```

where `G-` and `G+` are ordinary IsoGraph structures and `tau` is an extension-owned transition observation/claim whose anatomy is itself represented using ordinary IsoGraph structure.

DTS exists so that transition structure can participate in the same comparison, factorization, common-core, residual, provenance, QU, witness, and discovery machinery already used for state structure.

DTS does not require a transition to be executable, deterministic, causal, complete, sequential, or generated by a known rule.

---

# 2. One structural substrate

DTS MUST NOT create a second graph substrate.

```text
TransitionState
    = ordinary IsoGraph structure

TransitionAnatomy
    = ordinary IsoGraph structure interpreted through DTS roles
```

A DTS implementation may use internal indexes or normalized runtime objects, but semantic authority remains the represented IsoGraph structure plus pinned DTS/QU dependencies.

This homogeneity is load-bearing: it is what lets ordinary IsoGraph comparison see through transitions rather than treating them as opaque records.

---

# 3. Transition and projections

A transition `tau` relates a source state and a target state:

```text
pi-(tau) = G-
pi+(tau) = G+
```

The source and target projections identify the ordinary IsoGraph structures at the declared transition boundary.

A transition may be observed or asserted before its mechanism is known.

Therefore:

```text
Transition(tau, G-, G+)
```

does not imply:

```text
generatedBy(tau, known_rule)
```

or any particular causal interpretation.

---

# 4. Transition anatomy

Where the evidence and claim require it, transition anatomy may be factored into roles including:

```text
K          preserved structure
Delta-     removed structure
Delta+     introduced structure
Delta~     changed incidence / rewiring
B          boundary/interface
D          decomposition into subtransitions
O          ordering/dependency structure
E          evidence/provenance
Q          QU-bearing unresolved structure where applicable
```

These are semantic roles, not mandatory fixed-width fields and not independent primitive graph types.

A transition representation may omit a role only when the governing claim and closure authority make that omission legitimate.

## 4.1 Boundary reuse

`B` is ordinary IsoGraph boundary/interface structure interpreted in a transition role.

DTS introduces no second boundary notion.

## 4.2 Delta is source-faithful structure

`Delta-`, `Delta+`, and `Delta~` describe represented change under the pinned state/transition scope.

They MUST NOT silently claim that unobserved change did not occur unless the applicable closure authority supports that conclusion.

---

# 5. QU is the unresolved-transition substrate

QU 0.1 is a semantic dependency of DTS.

This does not require every transition artifact to materialize a QU state.

A determinate transition may contain no QU region when the applicable claim has sufficient closure authority.

When unresolved structure can affect a DTS claim, reconstruction, comparison, decomposition, TI result, or downstream interpretation, qualified QU semantics are REQUIRED.

DTS MUST NOT replace QU with private semantic statuses such as `UNKNOWN_MECHANISM`, `UNKNOWN_DELTA`, or `PARTIAL_TRANSITION` when the unresolved structure can be represented faithfully as QU in the relevant transition role.

The governing distinction is:

```text
no QU object present
    != transition known complete
```

For each load-bearing role, the claim must establish one of:

```text
fixed / sufficiently determined under authority
QU-represented
qualified irrelevant to this claim
```

Otherwise the claim is incomplete/unqualified at that boundary.

## 5.1 Claim-relative closure

Completeness is claim-relative.

A transition may be complete for an endpoint/delta claim while its internal decomposition remains QU-bearing.

The same unresolved decomposition becomes load-bearing when the comparison view asks about process topology.

Unknown structure may be projected away only when its irrelevance to the declared claim is itself qualified.

---

# 6. Decomposition

A transition may be decomposed into subtransitions:

```text
tau = tau1 o tau2 o ... o taun
```

or into a partial-order/dependency structure where a linear composition is not the represented semantics.

Multiple lawful decompositions may coexist.

DTS 0.1 does not define a canonical decomposition.

If decomposition topology is load-bearing in a TI claim, the compared decomposition representations and their authority MUST be pinned explicitly.

The base claim is therefore factorization-relative when `D` is load-bearing:

```text
TI_C(TA[DA], TB[DB])
```

DTS 0.1 does not yet assert existential or universal quantification over all admissible decompositions.

Such stronger claims may be investigated in later revisions only after evidence demonstrates that they are needed.

---

# 7. Transition Isomorph (TI)

A **Transition Isomorph (TI)** is a qualified IsoGraph structural correspondence between declared transition regions under a pinned comparison/view authority `C`.

```text
TI_C(TA, TB)
```

is a transition-structure claim, not an identity claim and not a mechanism claim.

A TI may include correspondence among any declared combination of:

```text
source-state roles
source/target boundary roles
K
Delta-
Delta+
Delta~
B
D
O
QU regions
other profile-owned structure admitted by the comparison view
```

A TI claim MUST identify which regions are load-bearing and which are projected/irrelevant under authority.

## 7.1 TI barriers

```text
TI
    != same endpoints
    != same mechanism
    != same rule
    != same action
    != same decomposition
    != same cost
    != same actual QU realization
    != NEI SAME
```

Structural correspondence can become evidence for another module only through that module's own authority.

## 7.2 TI evidence record

A material TI claim should preserve, as applicable:

```text
transition A
transition B
comparison/view authority C
included/load-bearing regions
projected/irrelevant regions + authority
pinned decomposition/factorization where load-bearing
QU dependencies
structural mapping/witness
residual structure
verification evidence
provenance + immutable revisions
```

A missing required QU or closure authority blocks the stronger TI claim; absence of represented difference is not positive TI evidence.

---

# 8. Worked TI example

This example is explanatory, not qualification evidence.

Suppose independently represented transitions `TA` and `TB` both transform a state with boundary roles `input` and `output`.

`TA` is decomposed into three observed subtransitions:

```text
read -> transform -> publish
```

`TB` is decomposed into two:

```text
specialized-transform -> publish
```

Both have corresponding source/target boundary structure and the same scoped structural effect:

```text
K          corresponding preserved context
Delta-     corresponding removed relation family
Delta+     corresponding introduced relation family
B          corresponding input/output boundary roles
```

Under comparison view `C_effect`, decomposition is explicitly projected as irrelevant.

A verified structural mapping over the included transition regions may therefore support:

```text
TI_C_effect(TA, TB)
```

while retaining `D` as residual structure.

Under comparison view `C_process`, decomposition and ordering are load-bearing.

The same pair need not satisfy:

```text
TI_C_process(TA, TB)
```

This demonstrates:

```text
scoped transition correspondence
    != global sameness of process
```

and:

```text
one TI view
    != TI under every comparison view
```

# 9. Discovery Protocol integration

Discovery Protocols may consume DTS structures exactly because transition anatomy remains ordinary IsoGraph structure.

Useful discovery targets include:

```text
state correspondence
transition correspondence
common delta structure
boundary correspondence
decomposition motifs
ordering motifs
QU/QUI correspondence inside transitions
TI candidates
TI residuals
cross-layer patterns
candidate invariants suggested by repeated transitions
```

Discovery priority or heuristic similarity never proves TI.

The intended path is:

```text
observe transition
-> preserve fixed + QU structure
-> factor only where justified
-> compare under pinned view
-> produce candidate correspondence
-> verify witness/residual
-> falsify competing interpretation
-> establish or reject TI
```

---

# 10. Role factorization is not invariant decomposition

The DTS anatomy roles organize transition facts by semantic role.

They are not automatically invariants.

An invariant is a property proved preserved under a declared comparison/transformation authority.

A future Transition Structural Signatures (TSS) profile may define cheap qualified signatures over roles such as `K`, deltas, `B`, `D`, `O`, and `Q` for candidate filtering.

For any such qualified invariant signature `Sigma_C`:

```text
Sigma_C(TA) != Sigma_C(TB)
    -> not TI_C(TA, TB)
```

but:

```text
Sigma_C(TA) = Sigma_C(TB)
```

only means that the TI candidate survives that filter.

DTS 0.1 itself defines no invariant vocabulary.

---

# 11. Mechanism and analysis profiles

DTS 0.1 deliberately leaves mechanism and analysis profiles outside base semantics.

Candidate profile families include:

```text
sequential/execution-trace profile
DPO rewrite profile
concurrency/partial-order profile
transition structural signatures profile
cost/accounting profile
optimization profile
external translation/adapters
```

Each profile is separately versioned and qualified.

Profile qualification MUST NOT be silently counted as DTS base qualification.

---

# 12. Anti-circularity

A desired TI result MUST NOT choose the comparison view, QU restriction, decomposition, projection, or mechanism interpretation later cited as proof of that TI.

Forbidden shape:

```text
want TI
-> choose decomposition that matches
-> project mismatching unresolved regions
-> claim TI
```

The load-bearing view, QU authority, and factorization/decomposition evidence must be independently pinned relative to the target claim.

Exploratory views may be recorded as hypotheses but are not qualified TI evidence until their authority burden is discharged.

---

# 13. Qualification targets

Before DTS 0.1 promotion, fresh qualification should demonstrate at least:

1. ordinary Core structures remain valid without DTS semantics;
2. source and target projections are recoverable;
3. transition anatomy is ordinary IsoGraph structure rather than a second substrate;
4. `B` reuses existing Core/QU-compatible boundary/interface structure;
5. a determinate transition does not require an artificial QU instance;
6. unresolved load-bearing transition structure requires QU rather than omission or a private DTS unknown token;
7. missing required QU/closure authority blocks the stronger claim;
8. identical endpoints do not establish TI;
9. a valid TI is recoverable under a pinned comparison view with explicit residuals;
10. decomposition differences do not cause false TI rejection where decomposition is independently qualified irrelevant;
11. decomposition topology affects TI when explicitly load-bearing;
12. QU-bearing TI preserves the distinction between structural correspondence and equality of actual QU realizations;
13. TI does not imply NEI SAME;
14. profile semantics do not leak into DTS base qualification;
15. a seductive false transition correspondence is rejected;
16. a target TI cannot circularly choose its own QU restriction, decomposition, or projection evidence;
17. incomplete observation cannot masquerade as complete transition structure merely because no QU object is present.

These are qualification obligations, not prescribed algorithms.

# 14. Working constitutional summary

```text
IsoGraph represents structure.
QU represents the constrained structure of what remains unresolved.
DTS represents structure through change.

A transition is an observation before it is a mechanism explanation.
Transition states and transition anatomy remain ordinary IsoGraph structure.
Unknown transition structure uses QU.
No QU instance is required when the relevant transition claim is genuinely determinate.
Absence of QU does not prove completeness.

Multiple decompositions may coexist.
Factorization is not canonicalization.
Role factorization is not invariant decomposition.

TI is scoped structural correspondence between transition regions.
Same endpoints are not TI.
TI is not mechanism identity, cost equality, QU-realization equality, or NEI SAME.

Mechanism and optimization machinery live in separately qualified profiles.
```
