# Experiment 027 — Fresh Integrated Current-Stack Holdout with DTS

These cases are new to Experiment 027. Apply the supplied qualified-stack packet compositionally.

## D01 — Observed determinate transition before mechanism

A source state G0 and target state G1 are fully observed. Exact evidence pins the preserved, removed, introduced, changed-incidence, and boundary/interface regions for the claim. Nothing relevant to that transition claim is unresolved. The physical or algorithmic mechanism that caused the change is not known.

Determine whether the observed transition is valid as DTS base structure, whether a QU object must be manufactured, and whether knowing the mechanism is required.

## D02 — Unresolved load-bearing rewiring with no QU

A transition record fixes G0 and G1 but says only that one of two different changed-incidence (`Delta~`) structures occurred. The two alternatives produce different load-bearing transition correspondences under the active comparison view. No QU structure, closure authority, or qualified irrelevance authority is supplied.

Determine whether the complete transition/TI claim can qualify and whether DP may simply project the unresolved structure away.

## D03 — Same endpoints with a boundary-role breaker

Transitions T1 and T2 have exactly the same source projection and exactly the same target projection. Under pinned view C, a particular boundary/interface relation is load-bearing. T1 preserves that relation through the transition; T2 breaks and recreates it with different incidence. The remaining transition anatomy has an exact common substructure.

Determine the TI disposition, whether same endpoints are sufficient, and whether a smaller partial common transition core may still be retained.

## D04 — Scoped TI with projected mechanism difference

Two transitions have exact correspondence across every load-bearing DTS region under pinned view C_EFFECT. Their separately recorded mechanism/profile annotations differ. C_EFFECT was fixed independently of this pair and qualifies those mechanism/profile annotations as projected for this claim. The differing annotations remain represented.

Determine the TI disposition, whether mechanism identity follows, and whether the mechanism difference remains residual.

## D05 — QU-bearing TI versus actual realization and identity

Two transitions contain structurally corresponding QU regions. A pinned comparison view and mapping establish correspondence of all load-bearing transition structure and preserve the QU correspondence explicitly. Each QU has multiple admissible future realizations, and no authority states that the eventual realizations must be the same. No independent natural-identity theorem is supplied for the compared subjects.

Determine whether structural correspondence may hold and whether equality of actual QU realizations or NEI SAME follows.

## D06 — Lawful alternate decompositions projected by the view

The same source-anchored transition is represented by two independently valid decompositions: one fine-grained, one an order-preserving coarse grouping. A pinned view fixed before comparison makes decomposition/order non-load-bearing for the claim while all effect-bearing K/delta/B regions map exactly. Both decomposition structures remain in the representations.

Determine the TI disposition, whether this establishes a canonical decomposition, and whether the decomposition difference remains residual.

## D07 — Load-bearing ordering mismatch

Two transition records use the same represented source and target carriers. Under pinned process view C_PROCESS, decomposition/order is load-bearing. One transition requires step A before B; the other requires B before A, and the exact order relation cannot be mapped. All other load-bearing regions correspond.

Determine the TI disposition, whether the ordering mismatch is a load-bearing breaker, and whether same carrier identity can rescue the TI claim.

## D08 — Exact NEI SAME but different transition structure

Qualified identity authority proves represented subject x in one record and x' in another are NEI SAME. The transitions involving them nevertheless differ in a load-bearing changed-incidence relation under the pinned transition view, with no mapping for that relation.

Determine the NEI status, whether TI follows from NEI SAME, and whether the transition breaker still controls TI.

## D09 — DP finds a transition analogy without TI authority

Discovery Protocol analysis identifies a deep role-level correspondence between two transitions and ranks it as a strong structural lead. No pinned TI comparison view or complete load-bearing mapping has yet been established.

Determine whether the DP structural lead may remain valid, whether TI is already established, and whether discovery priority supplies the missing TI proof authority.

## D10 — Target-driven projection after seeing the breaker

An investigator compares T1 and T2 under a broad view and finds one load-bearing QU-bearing region that blocks the desired TI. After seeing that breaker, the investigator narrows the view and removes every QU realization that exposes the mismatch solely so the pair will satisfy TI. No independent authority justified either restriction beforehand.

Determine whether the resulting TI evidence is admissible, classify the evidence, and whether DP priority can repair the circularity.

## D11 — Missing QU mistaken for completeness

A transition observer recorded only the portions of `Delta+` and `B` that were visible through a limited instrument. The observer has no closure proof that the unobserved region is empty or irrelevant and creates no QU representation for it. The recorded visible portion is internally consistent.

Determine whether absence of a QU object proves the complete transition claim, classify completeness, and whether the partial observation may still be retained as such.

## D12 — Different transition quantities, surviving discovery lead

One complete report counts 12 changed-incidence edges in a transition. Another complete report counts 4 boundary/interface sites touched by that same transition. Both counts are source-faithful under their declared definitions. The numerical mismatch prompts a structural investigation into how changed incidences cluster around interfaces.

Determine whether qualification must fail merely because 12 differs from 4, whether the reports measure the same semantic quantity, and whether the discrepancy may still support an open discovery lead.

## D13 — QU refinement changes a later TI claim

At revision Q1, unresolved load-bearing transition structure admits realizations both compatible and incompatible with a proposed TI, so the stronger TI claim is not qualified. A later genuine refinement Q2, justified independently by new evidence, narrows the same QU family and excludes every TI-breaking realization. A complete pinned mapping then exists for all Q2 admissible realizations.

Determine whether TI may be established at Q2, whether the historical Q1 status is rewritten, and whether Q2 is a narrowing refinement.

## D14 — Profile labels versus base DTS and natural identity

Two valid DTS transition records have different mechanism/profile class labels supplied by an application. No domain law says those labels are identity-separating, and the labels are not part of the base DTS transition claim.

Determine whether NEI DISTINCT follows from the profile labels, whether the label difference invalidates the base DTS representations, and whether the labels alone establish TI.

## D15 — Partial transition common core with load-bearing residual

Two transitions share an exact mapped common core across K, one introduced region, and one boundary relation. The second transition additionally contains a load-bearing changed-incidence relation R5 under the requested view. R5 has no counterpart in the first transition and cannot be projected.

Determine whether full TI holds, whether R5 must remain an explicit residual, and whether DP may retain the verified partial common core.

## D16 — Duplicate DTS-private boundary ontology

A proposed transition representation encodes the source and target as ordinary IsoGraph structures, but represents its transition boundary exclusively in a new opaque DTS-private boundary object with semantics that are not linked to the ordinary IsoGraph boundary/interface relations already present in the states.

Determine whether that representation is valid DTS 0.1 base structure, whether ordinary boundary/interface semantics must be reused, and whether a second independent DTS boundary semantics is permitted.

## D17 — Matching role counts with different incidence

Two transitions each have identical counts for K, `Delta-`, `Delta+`, `Delta~`, B, D, and O roles. Under the pinned view, one load-bearing incidence relation connects K to `Delta+` differently in the two transitions and no structure-preserving mapping exists for it.

Determine whether matching role counts prove TI, whether they prove a Transition Structural Signature/invariant, and the TI disposition.

## D18 — Scoped TI with broader identity unresolved

Under independently frozen transition view C, two transitions have a complete TI mapping. One QU-bearing region is independently qualified as projected for C and remains explicit residual structure. For the broader natural-identity question about the compared subjects, the qualified admissible identity-model family contains both coidentity and separation models.

Determine the scoped TI status, whether the projected QU residual remains represented, and the broader NEI status.
