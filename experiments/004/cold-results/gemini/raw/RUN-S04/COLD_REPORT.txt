## 5. Architectural Separation of Residuals (The Isolation Rule)

### 5.1 Mandated Verdict on Merging Residuals
**Verdict:** **NO.** Under no circumstances may a source-local D residual be merged into the pairwise comparison residual during end-to-end analysis.

### 5.2 Technical Justification
Section 19.4 of the Core Specification explicitly rules:
> "The source-local D/extraction records... own their own residual, information loss, provenance, discarded boundary/context... Pairwise comparison operates on the frozen comparison-stage objects... and owns a different accounting layer... A verifier MUST NOT satisfy source reconstruction by merging D-local residuals with pairwise residuals."

1.  **Semantic Boundary Violation:** A-side comparison residual ($R_{\text{pair\_A}}$) contains structure that was *retained* at the comparison stage but *did not match* the B-side under comparison policy. A-side local D residual ($R_{D(A)}$) contains structure that was *stripped* before the comparison stage even existed. Merging them collapses the critical distinction between "unmatched at comparison stage" and "unextracted at factorization stage."
2.  **Mapping Domain Incoherence:** The pairwise mapping $A \to B$ operates strictly over the comparison-stage objects' identity domain. The source-local residuals may contain arbitrary source-theory structural identities (e.g., node `4` in $S_{1000}$, node `8` in $S_{2000}$) that have never been mapped, registered, or evaluated for cross-domain correspondence. Merging them pollutes the domain of mapped structures.
3.  **Verification Failure:** Deterministic verification of the comparison-stage witness requires checkable, local mathematical bijections on frozen structures. Merging residuals violates boundary containment and breaks the mechanical checker's ability to verify the pairwise common core on the frozen comparative nodes alone.

### 7.2 Reconstruction Sufficiency Evaluation
**Verdict:** **INSUFFICIENT.** The lossy-discard summary `^6408` is structurally insufficient to reconstruct the original omitted incidences.

**Reasoning:**
The discard summary lists only the stable label/role categories that were omitted: `^7001` and `^7010`. Crucially, it does not record the *incidence topologies* or the *structural identities* associated with those relations (i.e., it has discarded the tuples `(2 3)` and `(3 4)`). 

From the summary `[^7001 ^7010]`, it is mathematically impossible to determine:
*   Which nodes were connected by relations `^7001` and `^7010`.
*   How many occurrences of these relations existed.
*   Whether any fresh or existing structural identities were referenced.

Therefore, the original source object `1000` cannot be reconstructed using this branch. The lossy indicator `#0` is accurate.

### 7.3 Inter-stage Upgrading Prohibition
**Verdict:** **NO.** Complete pairwise accounting at the comparison stage **cannot** convert this lossy D branch into a full-source reconstruction claim.

**Reasoning:**
Even if the comparison-stage object `1400` achieves a 100% complete, zero-residual pairwise match (Common Core) with another comparison-stage object, that pairwise match operates strictly on the comparison domain. 

The information lost during the $1000 \xrightarrow{D(1300)} 1400$ step was discarded *prior* to comparison. Since the pairwise comparison engine has access only to the frozen comparison objects, it contains no semantic authority to synthesize or recover the specific omitted local incidence details `(^7001 2 3)` and `(^7010 3 4)`. Under Section 19.4: "A lossy D/projection/quotient never becomes full-source reconstruction merely because the pairwise accounting is complete." Any full-source reconstruction claim for Source `1000` through the D `1300` branch is permanently invalidated and must be rejected by the verifier.
