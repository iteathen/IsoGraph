## 4. Distinguishing Round-Trip from Semantic-Decomposition Coverage

Lossless round-trip capability (recoverability) and semantic-decomposition coverage are **not** the same qualification property. They represent independent axes of decomposition evaluation:

1. **Lossless Round-Trip (Recoverability):** A conservation metric ensuring no information is destroyed during transform $D$. It is satisfied whenever:
   $$\text{Source} \equiv \text{Reconstruct}(\text{Target}, \text{Map}, \text{Residual})$$
2. **Semantic-Decomposition Coverage:** An analytical metric evaluating how much load-bearing structure is mapped out of the opaque source domain and translated into the visible, comparable target domain.

Factorization `9200` proves this distinction: it maintains **100% lossless round-trip recoverability** while delivering **0% semantic-decomposition coverage**.

## 5. Prohibition of Local Residual Leakage into Pairwise Accounting

**Verdict:** No residual in this source-local D audit may be reclassified as a pairwise common-core residual.

### Justification under Draft 0.15 rules (Section 19.4):
* **Strict Separation of Accounting Layers:**
  > *"The source-local D/extraction records own their own residual, information loss, provenance, discarded boundary/context, transparent reconstruction information, and exact round-trip witness... Pairwise comparison operates on the frozen comparison-stage objects... and owns a different accounting layer..."*
* **Anti-Cheating Enforcement:**
  > *"A verifier MUST NOT satisfy source reconstruction by merging D-local residuals with pairwise residuals."*

Reclassifying the source-local reconstruction residual of `9200` as a pairwise residual would mask the fact that the source structure was never actually decomposed. It would allow a lossy or vacuous decomposition to masquerade as an active pairwise structural common core during comparison, directly violating the isolation boundary.
