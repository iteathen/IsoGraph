## 4. Semantics of Equal Hashes within an Algorithm (Task 4)

### 4.1 What Equal Hashes May Mean
Under Section 32.3 of the Core Specification, a fingerprint/hash is a projection of a structural stage. Because algorithms `8100` and `8101` are qualified as invariants (`^6813 ... #1`), they guarantee the following implication:
$$\text{Isomorphic}(A, B) \implies f(A) = f(B)$$
Thus, when an algorithm produces equal hashes for Object `1000` and Object `2000` (such as `hash_8100(1000) == hash_8100(2000) == #111`), it means:
1.  The necessary condition for structural isomorphism under the given view (V0) and stage (`RAW_STAGE`) has been successfully satisfied.
2.  The candidate pair cannot be pruned as structurally distinct by this indexing filter.

### 4.2 What Equal Hashes Do Not Prove By Themselves
Hash equality is **non-evidential for positive structural isomorphism** in isolation. Specifically, it does not prove:
1.  **Absence of Hash Collisions (Lossy Projection):** A serializer/hash function is a projection into a limited space. It cannot guarantee injectivity (uniqueness) over all arbitrary graphs unless a "no-collision" complete representative certificate is verified. Two non-isomorphic structures can mathematically collide and yield the same hash.
2.  **Explicit Structural Witness/Mapping:** A hash match is an indexing shortcut. It does not supply the mandatory node-to-node and edge-to-edge bijective correspondence (the structural witness) required to satisfy an independent verifier (Section 29).
3.  **Cross-Algorithm Equivalence:** Equal hashes in one algorithm carry no authority over other algorithms unless a qualified cross-algorithm bridge is explicitly declared.
