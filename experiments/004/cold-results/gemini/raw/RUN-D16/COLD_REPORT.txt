## 5. Parameter Size vs. Vacuity Analysis

A critical question is whether a class schema should be rejected because its required structural pattern is small while its assigned parameter values are structurally large.

*   **Rule (Section 26.3 & Contract Section 6):** "Parameters may be structurally large. Size alone is neither evidence for nor against non-vacuity."
*   **Analysis:** In the assignments `1701` and `1702`, the assigned substructures contain 6 edges, whereas the schema pattern defines a constraint spanning only 2 edges. This structural divergence does not degrade the non-vacuity of Schema `1602`. 
*   **Verdict:** Parameter size alone does **not** decide vacuity. A class schema is non-vacuous if it constrains its parameter slot with a testable structural invariant that successfully rejects invalid shapes. Schema `1602` remains non-vacuous because its constraint is selective, whereas `1601` remains vacuous regardless of whether its assigned parameters are large (like `1710` with 6 edges) or small (like `1711` with 1 edge).
