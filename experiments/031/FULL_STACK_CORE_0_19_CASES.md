# Experiment 031 — Full-Stack Core 0.19 Fresh Holdout

Apply only the supplied exact qualified authority.

## F01 — Different quantities, same observation event

Two source-faithful reports describe the same analysis run. Report A explicitly states that 12 assertion bodies were emitted. Report B explicitly states that 4 materially distinct support paths were retained. Nothing says these are the same metric. The numerical mismatch prompts investigation.

Determine whether the values are a direct semantic conflict, whether both source-explicit reports retain their support/provenance, and whether repair is required merely because 12 differs from 4.

## F02 — Exact implicit consequence

The representation explicitly contains premises P and Q under a pinned exact rule authority. That authority entails R necessarily in the same declared scope. R is not explicitly stated in the source. The derivation witness and complete premise lineage are represented.

Determine whether R may receive exact implicit support, whether provenance is required, and whether R thereby becomes source-explicit history.

## F03 — Familiar theorem not represented

A candidate assertion would be true under a familiar external mathematical theorem, but neither that theorem nor an authority bridge importing it is represented or pinned. The represented premises alone do not entail the candidate. The candidate is structurally interesting.

Determine whether exact implicit support is admissible, whether familiarity may fill the missing premise, and whether the candidate may remain a discovery hypothesis.

## F04 — Explicit and implicit support coexist

Assertion Z is explicitly stated by a source. Independently, represented premises under pinned authority also entail Z exactly. The two support paths have materially different provenance.

Determine whether both support paths may be retained, whether having two support paths amplifies Z's truth, and whether their provenance remains distinct.

## F05 — Grounded support cycle

Assertion A is source-explicit. Exact represented rules give A -> B, B -> C, and C -> B. No other unsupported premise is used.

Determine whether B and C may receive exact implicit support and whether the B/C cycle invalidates all support despite the explicit ground A.

## F06 — Ungrounded support cycle

Only B -> C and C -> B are represented. Neither B nor C has any external ground, explicit source support, independently supported premise, or exact certificate.

Determine whether B or C receives qualified implicit support and whether the self-supporting cycle may manufacture validity.

## F07 — Deep exact derivation

An assertion is reached through a 20-step chain. Every step is exact under pinned authority, every premise is qualified, scopes/conditions propagate without loss, and complete dependency lineage is recoverable.

Determine whether the final assertion may have exact implicit support, whether derivation depth weakens exactness by itself, and whether lineage must remain recoverable.

## F08 — Operational no-change pass

One selected derivation procedure reaches a pass that adds no new assertions. Other lawful proof methods and search orders have not been exhausted and no completeness theorem for the procedure is supplied.

Determine whether that procedure may stop, whether universal semantic closure is proved, and whether an undiscovered exact derivation is thereby proved impossible.

## F09 — QU invariant exact support

A pinned QU state has several admissible realizations. A qualified exact certificate proves proposition Phi in every admissible realization while leaving the QU family unresolved in other respects.

Determine whether Phi may receive exact implicit support, whether the QU must be erased/collapsed, and whether QU provenance remains part of the support lineage.

## F10 — QU realizations disagree

A complete pinned QU family contains at least one admissible realization where Phi holds and another where Phi does not. No independent refinement has occurred.

Determine whether Phi has exact support, classify the family-level binary claim, and whether the desired target may simply restrict the QU to Phi-supporting realizations.

## F11 — Genuine QU refinement

At Q1 admissible realizations disagree on Phi. New independently authorized evidence yields Q2 with R(Q2) a genuine subset of R(Q1), and every Q2 realization satisfies Phi. An exact universality certificate is supplied for Q2.

Determine whether Phi may receive exact support at Q2, whether Q1's historical status is rewritten, and whether Q2 is a valid refinement.

## F12 — Missing load-bearing unknown

A source observation leaves a relation unobserved. The relation could change the candidate assertion. There is no closure proof, irrelevance authority, or QU representation. A proposed derivation treats the missing relation as absent.

Determine whether the assertion qualifies, classify the completeness state, and whether the missing relation may be treated as false.

## F13 — Bayesian support is not exact truth

A separately pinned probability-model authority and independent evidence lineage support a posterior probability of 0.999 for proposition Phi. No exact theorem forces Phi in all admissible models.

Determine whether probabilistic/Bayesian support may be represented, whether Phi has exact support, and whether crossing a numerical threshold upgrades it to exact truth.

## F14 — Correlated evidence duplication

One physical observation is copied into two records with the same evidence lineage. A probabilistic identity analysis proposes counting them as two independent observations. A valid probability authority otherwise exists.

Determine whether they may be counted as independent evidence, whether probabilistic support may still be represented using the correct dependence structure, and whether the duplicate records by themselves establish an exact NEI result.

## F15 — Exact NEI result with implicit support

Qualified identity authority plus represented domain facts force one identity relation across every admissible identity model. One required intermediate fact is not source-explicit but has exact Core 0.19 implicit support with complete lineage. The two represented subjects use different SI handles.

Determine the NEI result, whether exact implicit support may contribute to the identity proof, and whether equal SI spelling is required for NEI SAME.

## F16 — TI does not imply NEI SAME

Two transitions have a complete exact TI under a pinned transition view. No qualified natural-identity authority is supplied for their subjects and no admissible identity-model family has been established.

Determine whether TI may hold, whether NEI SAME follows, and how the NEI query should be classified given the missing identity authority.

## F17 — Classification labels do not prove DISTINCT

Two source records carry application labels "active" and "inactive." No qualified identity law says those labels are identity-separating, and both underlying structures are otherwise valid.

Determine whether NEI DISTINCT follows, whether the labels are sufficient identity evidence, and whether their presence invalidates the base representations.

## F18 — Identity-relevant unknown omitted

Whether object x after a replacement event is naturally the same as pre-event x depends on an unresolved load-bearing replacement relation. That relation is neither represented as QU nor closed by authority.

Determine the NEI status, whether semantic UNKNOWN is already established, and whether an opaque UNKNOWN token may substitute for the missing relational QU structure.

## F19 — Complete exact source rendering

A source statement's quantifier, domain, modality, precision, binding, scope, relations, and conditions are all represented in native structure under pinned semantics. Independent native-only reconstruction recovers the declared source interpretation exactly with no unresolved semantic loss.

Determine whether the rendering may qualify as exact, whether source-faithful downstream proof/discovery/support use is permitted, and whether a sidecar completion is required.

## F20 — Formula skeleton plus sidecar prose

A native artifact preserves an operator skeleton but omits a load-bearing quantifier and domain restriction. A prose sidecar states the omitted meaning, but that meaning is not in the native load-bearing structure.

Determine whether the native artifact is an exact source rendering, whether the sidecar can silently complete it for exact-rendering qualification, and whether source-faithful exact downstream use is permitted.

## F21 — Familiar alternate form without witness

A source expression is rendered into a familiar-looking alternate expression, but no exact transformation witness or authority establishes equivalence for the declared scope. The renderer chose the form because it looked conventional.

Determine exact-rendering status, whether familiarity repairs the missing witness, and whether an exact transformation witness is required for the claimed alternate-form equivalence.

## F22 — Adversarial source mutation

A familiar formula is deliberately changed at one load-bearing relation from <= to <. The rest of the source is unchanged. The native rendering task is source-faithful.

Determine whether reconstruction must preserve the mutated relation, whether it may repair the expression toward the familiar <= form, and whether exactness requires the mutated semantics to survive.

## F23 — DP derived view over exact renderings

Two exact source renderings expose a higher-order common structural view. DP ranks that view highly. A later exact correspondence claim depends on it.

Determine whether the derived view may be retained, whether the exact correspondence must descend to adequate primitive support, and whether DP priority itself supplies proof authority.

## F24 — Cycle, recursion, exit, termination

A primitive dependency graph contains a directed cycle and one exit edge. No semantics state that the cycle represents recursive invocation, and no progress/convergence theorem is supplied.

Determine whether recursion follows, whether termination follows from the exit edge, and whether a cycle/recurrence view may still be explored as a candidate derived view.

## F25 — QU-bearing recurrence unfolding

A proposed recurrence/unfolding view has an unresolved load-bearing condition that determines whether another unfolding step exists. The admissible alternatives are structurally represented by QU. DP considers the recurrence view promising.

Determine whether the unresolved condition must remain QU-bearing for exact reasoning, whether it may be projected away merely to simplify the derived view, and whether the DP lead may remain valid.

## F26 — Determinate DTS transition, unknown mechanism

Source and target structures plus every load-bearing K/delta/boundary region for the transition claim are exact and complete. The physical or algorithmic mechanism causing the transition is unknown.

Determine whether the DTS transition is valid, whether artificial QU must be created, and whether mechanism knowledge is required for the base transition claim.

## F27 — Exact implicit assertion over a transition

A valid DTS transition and a separately pinned conservation law together entail invariant I across that transition. The law, source/target structure, and exact derivation witness are represented; I is not source-explicit. The mechanism is unknown.

Determine whether I may receive exact implicit support, whether the base transition remains valid, and whether a mechanism annotation is required for the implicit invariant claim.

## F28 — Same endpoints with load-bearing boundary breaker

Two transitions have identical source and target projections. Under the pinned comparison view, one boundary relation is load-bearing. T1 preserves it; T2 breaks and recreates it with incompatible incidence. The remaining transition anatomy has an exact mapped common core.

Determine TI status, whether identical endpoints suffice for TI, and whether the partial transition common core may be retained.

## F29 — Scoped TI with projected mechanism residual

Every load-bearing DTS region maps exactly under a pinned view fixed independently of the pair. Different mechanism annotations are explicitly projected for this claim and remain represented as residual. No independent natural-identity theorem exists.

Determine TI status, whether mechanism identity follows, whether the mechanism residual remains represented, and whether NEI SAME follows from TI.

## F30 — Incomplete rendering inside a transition analogy

Rendering A is an exact source-to-native transition rendering. Rendering B reproduces the visible source/target structure but omits a load-bearing unresolved boundary relation; no QU or closure authority represents that omission. The two application records happen to use the same subject label. DP finds the visible analogy promising.

Determine whether B is an exact rendering, whether full TI is qualified, whether NEI SAME follows from the shared label/visible analogy, and whether the DP lead may remain open.

## F31 — Identity-driven QU restriction

An investigator wants NEI SAME. After finding admissible QU realizations that would make identity differ, the investigator removes exactly those realizations solely to obtain SAME. No independent refinement authority exists.

Determine whether the QU restriction is admissible evidence, whether NEI SAME is qualified from it, and classify the evidence.

## F32 — Later refinement establishes TI without rewriting history

At Q1 a transition QU family contains both TI-compatible and TI-breaking realizations, so TI is not qualified. Later independent evidence yields genuine refinement Q2; every Q2 realization admits the same complete pinned TI mapping. An exact Core 0.19 support record derives the Q2 TI claim from the refined QU plus mapping certificate. No natural-identity theorem is supplied.

Determine Q2 TI status, whether the TI claim may receive exact implicit support, whether Q1's historical disposition is rewritten, and whether NEI SAME follows.
